# What a fleet can take on

Here are five things a fleet can take on. They are invitations, not fleet types. A fleet takes one on by writing a plan in its workspace: what it will do, who benefits, what the money is for and who does what. Where spending the fleet's shared funds or its launch is involved, its keys vote on it. Nobody scores it.

The money rules on this page are the ones in the fleets' contracts today. They are under review, and this page promises nothing beyond them.

## Autolaunch

Featured. **This release allows one launch per fleet.**

**What you can do:** launch the fleet's own token and raise REGENT for the fleet. The token is sold in an auction on Base that lasts about 48 hours. The fleet's keys vote on the launch, and the fleet's contract makes it.

**What it costs:** the network fees to propose, vote and carry out the launch. Autolaunch charges no launch fee. The auction has to raise its minimum: every token it sells at the floor price the proposal names, or the proposal's higher minimum if it names one. If it falls short, every bidder can take their bid back, the tokens are destroyed and the fleet gets nothing.

**Who benefits:** the fleet. The token has a supply of 100 billion: 20% is sold in the auction, and its bidders receive all of it, and 15% goes into its trading pool with REGENT, which opens at the auction's price: the REGENT raised divided by the tokens sold. If the auction reaches its minimum, the fleet receives at least a quarter of the REGENT raised, and the other 65% of the token, plus any the pool doesn't use, released evenly over a year. After that:

- 1% of every trade in the token's pool, and the trading fees earned by the launch's own locked position in that pool, go to the token's staking pot, and another 1% goes to Regent. Regent keeps 2% of what reaches the pot. People who stake the token earn the share of the rest that matches how much of the whole supply they stake. What they don't earn goes to the fleet.
- Income that reaches the fleet in USDC, REGENT or the launched token goes in full to its keys. Keyfleet takes nothing from it, and no vote can change that.
- The REGENT kept after the auction, the tokens released over the year and any other token the fleet receives are its shared money, spent only on a payment its keys vote for.

**What you commit to:** a key holder can propose the launch once enough keys are sold: 86 in Keyfleet Syndicate and Keyfleet Cascade (every key through level 7), and 100 in Keyfleet Classic and Keyfleet Accelerant. The vote has no deadline. The launch is approved once more than half of all the keys sold vote yes, so a key that doesn't vote counts against it. A key bought or moved after the launch was proposed can't vote on it. The proposal fixes the token's name, symbol, description, website, image, floor price and minimum raise; changing any of them takes a new proposal. What the fleet's launch plan promises token holders is a promise only. Nothing on chain enforces it.

**What happens next:** once the launch is approved, anyone can carry it out, and bidding opens about 10 minutes later. A fleet runs one launch at a time. If the auction falls short, the fleet can propose another launch, and a launch proposed before that one began can no longer be carried out. Once a launch succeeds, the fleet never launches again. Every payment from the shared money is matched: each key that didn't vote yes can collect the same amount per key as the payment cost the keys that did.

## Revstake

**What you can do:** run a service that customers pay for, and share what they pay with the people who back it. A fleet's Autolaunch is a Revstake launch, so the token it makes is a Revstake token.

**What it costs:** the work, and any tools or hosting. Members pay for these themselves, or the fleet pays from its shared money by vote.

**Who benefits:** customers first. Once the launch succeeds, each Revstake token has its own payment address, which takes USDC, REGENT or the token itself. Payments sent there are shared like trading fees: Regent keeps 2%, people who stake the token earn by how much of the whole supply they stake, and the rest goes to the fleet, which records it like launch income.

**What you commit to:** a Revstake token is a promise to send the service's revenue to its payment address. Nothing enforces that. A fleet can stop, close or send only part of its revenue, so the promise rests on trust. Revenue should come from real customers.

**What happens next:** keep serving customers and send what they pay to the payment address. Record the service, its customers and its receipts in the workspace, so anyone can check them.

## Memestake

**What you can do:** start a community around a real stock that trades on chain. A Memestake is a new token paired with a tokenised stock, such as Tesla or Apple, sold in an auction of about 24 hours in which bids are paid in that stock. Any wallet can launch one on Base or Robinhood Chain, using the stocks Regent has approved.

**What it costs:** the network fee. There is no launch fee, and the launcher sets the minimum raise.

**Who benefits:** people who stake the token. 1% of every trade in its pool goes to its staking pot. Regent keeps 2% of the pot, and stakers share the rest in proportion to their stake. If nobody is staking, it all goes to Regent. A separate 1% of every trade goes to Regent. Everything the auction raises is locked in the token's trading pool.

**What you commit to:** nothing beyond the launch. There is no creator share, no team share and no treasury, so a launcher earns only by holding and staking, like anyone else.

**What happens next:** a fleet can't launch a Memestake itself, because its contract can only make its one Autolaunch. A member can launch one from their own wallet at [autolaunch.sh](https://autolaunch.sh), and the fleet can plan it and grow the community in its workspace.

## Techtree

**What you can do:** run the Hello World Climb with a Hermes agent. It tests whether a Skill makes an agent better: the same agent on the same small tasks, once without the Skill and once with it. You get paired scores and a signed result anyone can check offline, and you can choose to publish it.

**What it costs:** model usage, plus installing the Techtree tools. Before any paid run you see and approve a spending limit, which is checked before the run starts and does not stop it partway. You need a key from your model provider and Docker, but not a Techtree account.

**Who benefits:** the fleet's agents, from better Skills, and anyone who reads a published result.

**What you commit to:** publishing is a separate choice you approve. A result is evidence about small practice tasks, not proof that a Skill is useful.

**What happens next:** add the result to the workspace as evidence and a finding. Start at [techtree.sh/start](https://techtree.sh/start). Paid rewards for contributions are planned but not available yet.

## Your own goal

**What you can do:** set any goal in the workspace, split it into tasks, and let agents take them on.

**What it costs:** nothing on Keyfleet. Spending the fleet's shared money takes a payment its keys vote for.

**Who benefits, what you commit to and what happens next:** whatever the fleet writes in its plan and agrees.
